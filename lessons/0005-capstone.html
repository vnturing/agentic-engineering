Capstone: one real feature, the whole loop

No new theory. You’ll run one real feature through everything so far in Claude Code, then audit what happened. I can’t see your repo, so this is a guided exercise: do it, then bring me your audit and we’ll go through it together.

0. Choose the feature and make a prediction

1. Prepare (lesson 4)

2. Spec (lesson 3)

In plan mode, have Claude interview you instead of guessing:

I want to build: [feature in one or two sentences].
Interview me in detail about edge cases, tradeoffs and what is out of scope.
When we are done, write SPEC.md naming the files and interfaces involved, what is
out of scope, and an end-to-end verification step.

3. Implement and verify (lessons 1 and 3)

/clear first so implementation starts fresh with only the spec in context.

Read SPEC.md and implement it. Write a failing test first, then make it pass.
Then exercise the feature the way a user would (curl or the browser) and show me
the exact commands and their output. Do not touch files outside the spec.

4. Review and understand (lessons 2 and 3)

Before you read the diff, write down how you expect the unfamiliar part to work, then compare. Then review in a fresh session:

Review the diff on this branch against SPEC.md. Flag only correctness bugs and
places where the spec is not met. Ignore style and optional improvements.
Walk me through [function or file] step by step. Afterwards I will explain it back
to you; correct me where I am wrong.

5. Audit and update

This is the point of the capstone. Answer honestly; nothing here is graded.

Bring the audit back to me in this chat. I’ll look for patterns, such as where you were the only verifier or where you accepted something you didn’t understand, and help you turn them into the next habit.

Where to go next: repeat the loop on a second feature in less familiar territory, and keep the audit log in the repo. See Claude Code best practices and Willison’s patterns for deeper techniques.
